1. Origins: From an iPhone 3GS in 2011 to Modern WebGL2#

Fifteen years ago, for my software engineering thesis at ITBA (Instituto Tecnológico de Buenos Aires), I built iSurfer—an interactive algebraic surface renderer for the iPhone 3GS. Inspired by the IMAGINARY open-mathematics project and Oberwolfach's SURFER, the challenge in 2011 was brutal: mobile CPUs could not evaluate millions of polynomial ray intersections per frame, so I had to write a custom ray tracer inside dynamically compiled OpenGL ES 2.0 GLSL fragment shaders running on the PowerVR SGX535 GPU.

SurfZar (surfzar.vercel.app) brings that vision into the modern browser with Next.js, React 19, Three.js (React Three Fiber), and custom WebGL2 GLSL shaders—requiring zero installation, zero plugins, and zero backend compute.

2. Rendering Pure Implicit Zero-Sets f(x,y,z)=0f(x, y, z) = 0f(x,y,z)=0#

Unlike conventional 3D engines that approximate curved surfaces with triangle meshes, SurfZar directly renders continuous zero-level sets of implicit polynomial and transcendental equations in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3:

S={(x,y,z)∈R3∣f(x,y,z)=0}\mathcal{S} = \{(x, y, z) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid f(x, y, z) = 0\}S={(x,y,z)∈R3∣f(x,y,z)=0}

For every pixel on the screen, a viewing ray r(t)=o+td\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}r(t)=o+td is cast through a bounding volume (Box, Sphere, Cylinder, or Torus). Substituting the ray equation into f(x,y,z)=0f(x, y, z) = 0f(x,y,z)=0 reduces the 3D surface intersection problem to finding the smallest positive real root t>0t > 0t>0 of a univariate function g(t)=f(o+td)=0g(t) = f(\mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}) = 0g(t)=f(o+td)=0.

Solving Singularities and Thin-Sheet Artifacts#

When rendering classic algebraic surfaces like Steiner's Roman Surface or the Barth Sextic, two notorious GPU shader artifacts appear:

  1. Parabolic Vertex Halo Noise: Along self-intersecting axes where ∇f=0\nabla f = \mathbf{0}∇f=0, standard sign-change detectors miss grazing roots or trigger false positives. SurfZar validates local extrema and near-miss intervals before discarding or refining a root.
  2. Two-Sided Manifold Orientation (rayHitSign): Implicit surfaces partition space into interior (f(x,y,z)<0f(x,y,z) < 0f(x,y,z)<0) and exterior (f(x,y,z)>0f(x,y,z) > 0f(x,y,z)>0). By tracking the sign of fff along the approaching ray prior to root refinement, SurfZar prevents normal-flipping shimmer on thin sheets and shades Side A (Frontface) and Side B (Backface) with independent PBR materials, normal maps, metallic/roughness parameters, and HDRI environment reflections.

4. Parametric Timeline Animation & Watertight 3D Printing#

Beyond static inspection, SurfZar is a complete creative studio for mathematical visualization:

  • Multi-Track Keyframe Dope-Sheet: Animate parameters a,b,c,d∈[−5,5]a, b, c, d \in [-5, 5]a,b,c,d∈[−5,5] alongside continuous time TTT over a 0–10s timeline with linear, step, and smooth cubic interpolation, plus direct WebM video capture and 4K transparent PNG snapshots.
  • Dynamic Cross-Section Clipping: Slice through ±X,±Y,±Z\pm X, \pm Y, \pm Z±X,±Y,±Z axes in real time to inspect internal chambers inside minimal surfaces like the Gyroid (sin(x)cos(y) + sin(y)cos(z) + sin(z)cos(x) = 0).
  • Marching Cubes STL / OBJ Export: With a single click, SurfZar polygonizes the implicit field into a watertight triangle mesh ready for 3D printing in Bambu Studio, PrusaSlicer, Cura, or Blender.
  • Lossless URL State Serialization: Every equation, camera matrix, dual-sided PBR material, clipping plane, and keyframe track compresses into a shareable #share=... URL hash.
